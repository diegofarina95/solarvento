Frequently asked questions about solar panels and self-consumption

We answer the most common questions about solar self-consumption in Spain: how many panels you need, whether a battery is worth it, how much you save, the payback and the permits. Want figures for your case? Use the calculator with your bill.

How many solar panels do I need for my home?

It depends on your annual consumption, on the orientation and tilt of your roof and on the solar radiation in your area. As a reference, a home that consumes around 3,500 kWh per year is usually covered by 3 to 5 kWp, that is, about 7 to 12 panels of ~450 W. The precise way is to calculate it with your real bill: SolarVento estimates the power and the number of panels based on your consumption and your location.

How are solar panels calculated from the electricity bill?

Your bill shows how much energy you consume (kWh) and in which periods. With that consumption and the solar radiation of your location (data from PVGIS, from the European Commission), the power needed to cover a good part of your spending, the number of panels, the recommended battery and the savings are estimated. SolarVento reads this data automatically from the bill you upload.

Is a solar battery worth it?

A battery is worthwhile above all if you consume a lot at night or at the end of the day, when there is no longer any sun. It increases self-sufficiency and self-consumption, but it also makes the installation more expensive and lengthens the payback. If your consumption is during the day and you have good surplus compensation, it may not pay off. The calculator evaluates your specific case and tells you whether it is worth it for you.

How much can you save with solar panels?

The savings depend on your consumption, on how much energy you use while there is sun and on your tariff. In well-sized residential installations it is common to reduce between 40% and 70% of the energy portion of the bill. The exact figure for your case is obtained with the calculator.

How long does it take to pay off a solar installation?

The typical payback of a residential installation in Spain is usually between 5 and 9 years, depending on the cost, the consumption and the available subsidies. Since panels last 25-30 years, the rest of their useful life is savings. SolarVento calculates your estimated payback and ROI.

What is surplus compensation?

It is the mechanism by which the energy you produce and do not consume (the surplus) is fed into the grid and your electricity supplier discounts it from your bill. Under the simplified compensation scheme, the discount cannot exceed the cost of the energy you do consume: the energy bill can drop to zero, but not go negative. Any surplus above that is not paid for, except under specific sale contracts.

Do I need permits for self-consumption in Spain?

Small self-consumption installations have a simplified procedure, but they must be legalised: installation certificate, notification to the regional government and to the distributor, and registration under the surplus compensation scheme if applicable. This is normally handled by the installer. It is advisable to use an authorised installer.

What is peak power (kWp)?

The kWp (kilowatt-peak) is the maximum power that an installation can generate under standard conditions. It is the measure used to size the system: for example, an installation of 4 kWp with ~450 W panels would have about 9 panels.

How much energy does a solar panel produce per year in Spain?

In Spain, each kWp installed produces approximately between 1,400 and 1,700 kWh per year, depending on the area (more in the south, less in the north) and the orientation. SolarVento uses real radiation data from your location (PVGIS) to fine-tune the estimate.

SolarVento is free and requires no registration or account creation. You upload your bill or enter the data by hand and get the estimate instantly.

Your bill is processed only to estimate your installation and is not kept beyond your session. You have the details in the privacy policy.
