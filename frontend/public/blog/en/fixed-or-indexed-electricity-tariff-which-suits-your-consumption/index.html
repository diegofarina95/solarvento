Fixed or Indexed Electricity Tariff: Which One Suits Your Consumption?

9 July 2026

When you sign up for electricity, the first big decision is choosing between a fixed tariff and an indexed tariff. And there is no universal answer: what is best for your neighbour may be the worst option for you. It depends on three things —how much you value stability, whether you can shift your consumption, and how closely you want to track prices—. Let’s look at how each one works and which type of user it suits.

How each tariff works

The essential difference

  1. Fixed tariff — you pay a locked-in price per kWh, which does not change during the term of the contract. You know what you will pay no matter what happens in the market.
  2. Indexed tariff — you pay the real wholesale market price hour by hour, plus the supplier’s margin. It goes up and down every day; you follow the market in real time.

A fixed tariff is like a set-price menu: you pay what was agreed, even if the market soars or collapses. An indexed tariff is like paying the bill based on the price of the day: when the market is cheap, you win; when it spikes, you pay for it.

The fixed tariff: peace of mind in exchange for a small premium

With a fixed tariff, the supplier takes on the risk that the market price will rise, and in return charges you a slightly higher average price —that margin is its “insurance”—. It is the option for anyone who prioritises predictability: stable bills, no shocks, easy to budget for.

It works well if you feel stressed not knowing what you are going to pay, if you have a tight budget where one expensive month can throw everything off, or if you simply do not want to think about it. You pay a little more on average, but you buy peace of mind.

The indexed tariff: cheaper on average, but with ups and downs

An indexed tariff is usually cheaper in the long run, because you are not paying the “insurance” built into a fixed tariff. The trade-off is that you take on the risk: in a month with high prices, your bill goes up and there is nothing you can do about it.

The important detail is that an indexed tariff rewards anyone who can shift consumption to cheaper hours. Since the price changes every hour, if you concentrate the washing machine, dishwasher, electric water heater or EV charging in low-price hours, you get more out of the tariff. If you consume at any time without checking, you make less of its advantage.

The key question: can you and do you want to shift your consumption to cheaper hours, and cope calmly with the odd expensive month? If yes, an indexed tariff usually pays off. If you prefer to know exactly what you will pay each month and not have to monitor prices, a fixed tariff will give you more peace of mind even if it costs a little more.

Which profile each one suits

Which to choose based on your habits

  1. Fixed — if you value stability, have a tight budget or do not want to think about it. You pay a little more to avoid surprises.
  2. Indexed — if you can shift consumption to cheaper hours and tolerate variability. Cheaper on average if you make the most of it.
  3. Not sure — if you are unsure, the fixed tariff is the default “safe” option. Less optimisation, but zero surprises.

A couple of warnings before you decide

Watch out for two things. First, compare the real total price, not just the kWh price: some “cheap” tariffs hide the cost in fixed fees or added services. Second, check the contract lock-in: many fixed tariffs tie you in for a year, so if you regret it, switching may cost you.

And remember that the market changes. A fixed tariff that seems expensive today may pay off if prices shoot up; and an excellent indexed tariff one year may give you a nasty surprise the next. There is no perfect decision forever: there is only the one that fits your situation and your personality right now.

In short

A fixed tariff gives you predictable bills in exchange for a small premium; an indexed tariff is usually cheaper on average but comes with ups and downs, and rewards anyone who can shift consumption to cheaper hours. Choose fixed if you value peace of mind and do not want to monitor prices; choose indexed if you tolerate variability and take advantage of cheaper hours. In both cases, look at the total price, not just the kWh price.
