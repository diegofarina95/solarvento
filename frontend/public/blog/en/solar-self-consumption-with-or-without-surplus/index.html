Solar self-consumption with or without surplus: which option is best?

18 July 2026

When you start looking into solar panels, one of the first questions people throw at you is: “do you want it with surplus or without surplus?”. And for most people, that sounds like another language. It is one of the decisions that causes the most confusion at the beginning, so let’s clear it up once and for all, based on what the law says and with a practical recommendation so you know what to choose.

What the two options mean

The difference is simple once it is explained properly. It has to do with what happens to the energy your panels produce and you do not consume at that moment:

The two main types of self-consumption

  1. With surplus (con excedentes) — the energy you have left over is exported to the grid and you receive something in return. Your installation can inject the surplus into the public grid.
  2. Without surplus (sin excedentes) — a device prevents anything from being exported to the grid. It includes an “anti-export system” (sistema antivertido); whatever you do not consume at the time is wasted.

In other words: under the with surplus option, when you produce more than you use, that excess energy goes to the grid and you are compensated for it, usually as a discount on your bill. Under the without surplus option, a mechanism called anti-export blocks that injection: if at any given moment you produce more than you consume, that energy is simply lost; it does not go anywhere.

And within “with surplus”, there are two more variants

To make things a little more complicated, because Spanish regulations are like that, the with-surplus option is itself divided into two:

  • With surplus under compensation (con excedentes acogida a compensación): the usual option for homes. Your electricity supplier discounts the value of what you export from your bill. It requires the installation to be renewable and to have a power output of no more than 100 kW; every home easily meets this requirement.
  • With surplus NOT under compensation: designed for large installations or special cases, where instead of bill compensation the energy is sold to the market as a producer. For a normal home, it makes no sense: more hassle than benefit.

So, which one is best for me?

Here is the direct answer, without beating around the bush: for practically any home, the right option is “with surplus under compensation”. And the reason is pure common sense.

Why with surplus almost always wins: with solar panels, you will produce more than you consume at many times of day, especially around midday, when perhaps nobody is at home. If you choose “without surplus”, all that leftover energy is thrown away. If you choose “with surplus”, at least it is compensated on your bill. For the same installation, the with-surplus option ALWAYS gives you more savings, because it makes use of what you would otherwise lose.

So when does “without surplus” make sense?

The without-surplus option is a minority case, reserved for specific situations:

  • When you want to avoid paperwork as much as possible: an installation without surplus has slightly simpler processing, because it does not require a compensation contract. But the difference in paperwork is small and does not make up for losing your surplus energy.
  • When you consume almost everything you produce at the time: for example, a business with high, constant daytime consumption that generates hardly any surplus. In that case, losing the surplus barely hurts. But this is rare in a home.
  • Because of a specific technical or administrative restriction that prevents exporting to the grid at your connection point.

For a normal home, none of these cases usually apply. You are going to produce surplus one way or another, and you will want to be compensated for it.

A note on the paperwork

Good news to ease one concern: the with-surplus option is NOT a bureaucratic nightmare. For installations on urban land of up to 15 kW, meaning any home, complex access and connection permits are not required, and the process is highly simplified. All you need is a bidirectional meter, which measures what you consume and what you export, and a compensation contract with your electricity supplier. Your installer handles almost everything.

The recommendation in one sentence: unless you have a very specific reason to do otherwise, choose self-consumption with surplus under compensation. It is what suits the vast majority of homes, it makes the most of your installation, and the paperwork is straightforward. “Without surplus” is the exception, not the rule.

In summary

The difference between self-consumption with and without surplus lies in what happens to the energy you have left over: with surplus it is exported to the grid and compensated; without surplus, an anti-export system blocks it and it is lost. For almost any home, the right option is “with surplus under compensation”, because it uses surplus energy you would otherwise throw away, provides greater savings, and does not complicate the paperwork. The without-surplus option is for very specific cases. If in doubt, with surplus is almost always the answer.
