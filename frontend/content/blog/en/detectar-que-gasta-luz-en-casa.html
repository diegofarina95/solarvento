When the bill arrives higher than expected, the question is always the same: what on earth is using so much? The good news is you don’t need to buy any plug-in monitor to find out. With what you already have at home —your meter and your bill— and a bit of detective work, you can track down the culprit yourself.

Your best tool is already installed: the meter

The digital meter you have at home (or on the landing) is a real-time consumption meter, free and already installed. Most of them show, by pressing a button, the instantaneous power you are using at that moment, normally in kW. That number is your detective tool.

The idea is simple: if you look at that number, switch something off, and the number drops, you’ve just measured how much that thing was using. Repeat the move around the house and you’ll find the culprits without spending a euro.

How to read instantaneous power: on most digital meters, pressing the button several times takes you through different screens until you see the current power (a value in kW, such as "0.45 kW"). If you can’t find it, look up your meter model: almost all of them show it.

The baseline reading method

Start by finding out how much your home uses “at rest”, with everything you think is off:

  1. Switch off everything you can —lights, TV, appliances— leaving only what you can’t turn off (fridge, router, things plugged in on standby).
  2. Check the instantaneous power on the meter. That is your “baseline consumption”: what you use even when you’re doing nothing.
  3. If that number is high —say, well above what a fridge alone would use— it means you have hidden phantom consumption: appliances on standby, chargers, a poorly programmed water heater, something plugged in that you haven’t noticed.

That baseline consumption is revealing: it is what you pay for 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Lowering it, even a little, is multiplied by every hour of the year.

The appliance-by-appliance hunt

Now you go after the big picture. With the meter in sight:

The detective method, step by step

  1. Write down the power with everything at rest — your starting point. Example: 0.15 kW.
  2. Switch ONE appliance on and see how much it rises — the difference is what that appliance uses. The oven can push it up to 2 kW or more in one go.
  3. Switch it off and move on to the next one — one by one so you don’t mix things up. Water heater, ceramic hob, air conditioning, tumble dryer: the usual suspects.
  4. The ones that raise the number the most are your culprits — that’s where your bill is. Almost always, anything that produces heat or cold.

In one afternoon you’ll have a map of your home: you’ll know which appliances make the number shoot up and which barely move it. And you’ll almost always confirm the golden rule: anything that heats or cools (water heater, heating, air conditioning, oven) is what really matters, while electronics barely count.

Tracking down phantom consumption

Phantom consumption —appliances that use electricity even when they look switched off— is the slipperiest because you can’t see it. With the baseline reading method, you’ve already detected it if your at-rest consumption was high. To locate it:

  • Unplug power strips and standby appliances (TV, games console, microwave with a clock, chargers) while watching the meter. Anything that makes the number drop when you unplug it was using phantom power.
  • The usual suspects: equipment with an always-on power supply, old appliances, and anything with a little light or clock permanently on.
  • The solution is often a power strip with a switch so you can turn off a group of appliances you don’t use at night in one go.

Use your bill too

Your bill complements the investigation. Look at the breakdown of consumption by billing period (peak (punta), shoulder (llano), off-peak (valle)): if you use a lot in peak, you have expensive consumption during expensive hours and room to shift it. And compare bills from different months: a big jump from one month to the next tells you when the problem started —perhaps when you turned the heating on, or when the heat arrived and the air conditioning came on—.

The detective’s summary: the meter tells you WHAT is using electricity (appliance by appliance), and the bill tells you WHEN and in which time band. With both, you have the full map of your consumption, without having spent a euro on meters.

In short

You don’t need to buy anything to know what is driving up your electricity bill: your meter measures instantaneous power, and with the method of switching things on and off one by one, you can catch the culprits in an afternoon. First check your at-rest consumption to spot phantom loads, then go appliance by appliance, and cross-check the data with the period breakdown on your bill. You’ll almost always discover the same thing —anything that heats or cools is what really matters— but now with evidence, not guesswork.
