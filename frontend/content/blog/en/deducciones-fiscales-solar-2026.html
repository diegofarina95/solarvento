If you’re thinking about installing solar panels, 2026 is a very favourable year from a tax point of view: there are several Spanish personal income tax deductions that can return a significant part of your investment through your income tax return. But —and this is key— they have expiry dates and requirements that must be met in detail. Here’s what is available this year, with no hidden fine print.

Important notice: this article is general information, not tax advice. These deductions have technical requirements and deadlines that you should confirm with a tax adviser or directly with the Agencia Tributaria before making decisions. Figures and conditions may change; always check the rules currently in force for your specific case.

What’s new in 2026: a specific deduction for solar self-consumption

This year’s big change comes from Royal Decree-Law 7/2026, which has created a specific deduction for photovoltaic self-consumption installations carried out during 2026, in addition to extending the deductions that already existed. What makes this new deduction attractive is that it is simpler to apply than the older ones, because it does not require proving an efficiency improvement with energy performance certificates.

The new self-consumption deduction (RD 7/2026)

  1. 10% for individual homes — on a maximum base of €5,000 per year. Maximum deduction of €500. This is the one that applies to most houses.
  2. 20% for residential buildings — on the same €5,000 base. Maximum deduction of €1,000, for installations in residential-use buildings.
  3. Batteries count — storage systems are included in the base. Adding a battery to the installation also qualifies for tax relief.
  4. It is applied in the 2027 income tax return — the one you file the year after the installation. You install in 2026 and claim it in your 2027 tax return.

This deduction is especially relevant because it includes batteries, something the older deductions did not address clearly, and because it does not require the process of obtaining two energy performance certificates. In exchange, its percentages (10-20%) are more modest than those of the efficiency deductions.

The “big” energy efficiency deductions (extended)

In addition to the new deduction, the three deductions for improving energy efficiency remain in force in 2026. They offer much higher percentages —but they require you to prove an improvement with energy performance certificates before and after the works—:

The energy efficiency deductions (20%, 40%, 60%)

  1. 20% — if you reduce heating and cooling demand by at least 7%. Maximum base €5,000/year. The most accessible option.
  2. 40% — if you reduce non-renewable primary energy consumption by at least 30%, or achieve an A or B rating. Maximum base €7,500/year. The most common fit for a good solar installation.
  3. 60% — for comprehensive energy renovation of entire residential buildings. Base of €5,000/owner, up to €15,000 accumulated. Typical for homeowners’ communities.

The 40% deduction is usually the best fit for a well-designed solar installation in a home: if you can certify a 30% reduction in non-renewable primary energy consumption —something achievable with a decent installation— you can deduct 40% on a base of up to €7,500, i.e. up to €3,000. Much more than the 10% under the new deduction.

Which one is better for me, the new deduction or the efficiency deduction? You cannot claim both on the same investment. In general, the 40% efficiency deduction gives you much more back —up to €3,000 versus €500— but it requires two energy performance certificates and proof of the improvement. The new 10% deduction is simpler but gives less. Which one is worth it depends on your case and whether your installation achieves the required improvement: this is where a tax adviser or a good installer can guide you.

The requirements you cannot skip

  • You must be the owner of the home or property where the installation is carried out. (That is why, as we saw in another article, renting or leasing solar panels does not give access to these deductions.)
  • Energy performance certificates (for the 20/40/60% efficiency deductions): one before and one after the works, showing the improvement. The post-works certificate must be issued within the deadline set by the regulation.
  • Documentary proof: installation invoices with tax ID and itemised breakdown, plus proof of electronic payment (cash is not accepted). Without paperwork, the Agencia Tributaria will reject the deduction.
  • Deadlines: the deductions are linked to installations carried out during 2026 (with nuances depending on each category). That is the “clock” that is ticking.

And don’t forget regional and municipal incentives

State-level Spanish income tax deductions can be combined with grants and incentives from your autonomous community and your town hall, multiplying the savings:

  • Regional deductions: many autonomous communities have their own deductions, compatible with the state-level one, with percentages that in some cases reach 50%. They vary a lot depending on where you live.
  • IBI rebate: many town halls offer a rebate on the Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles for several years if you install self-consumption.
  • ICIO rebate: the tax on building works can be significantly reduced in many municipalities.
The key point right now: what makes 2026 especially attractive is the combination of the new self-consumption deduction, the extension of the efficiency deductions, and regional and municipal incentives, all at the same time. But the state-level deductions are tied to installations carried out this year: this is a framework with an expiry date, not something that will be available indefinitely.

In short

In 2026 you have, on the one hand, the new RD 7/2026 deduction (10% for homes, 20% for buildings, with batteries included and no need for certificates) and, on the other, the extended energy efficiency deductions (20%, 40% and 60%), which give much more back but require certificates proving the improvement. On top of that, there are regional grants and municipal IBI and ICIO rebates. All of this makes 2026 a very favourable tax year for self-consumption, but with deadlines that expire: you need to install within the tax year and keep all documentation. Always confirm your specific case with a tax adviser.

Before anything else, calculate whether solar makes sense for you and what size your installation should be: estimate your savings and payback using your bill, and then add the effect of these deductions on the investment.
